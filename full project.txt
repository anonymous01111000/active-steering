Activation Steering in Artificial Intelligence

1.Introduction
Recent advancements in Artificial Intelligence (AI) have yielded generative models capable of highly sophisticated linguistic output, increasingly blurring the distinction between human and artificial conversational agents. However, the appearance of cognitive awareness in Large Language Models (LLMs) is an emergent property of autoregressive next-token prediction over high-dimensional data distributions, rather than an indicator of genuine consciousness. Unlike human cognition, which is intrinsically bound to a biological substrate and modulated by the neurochemical cascades of the endocrine and nervous systems, AI lacks subjective physiological experience.
While foundational artificial neural networks were conceptualized through biological biomimicry, modern LLM architectures diverge fundamentally from human neurobiology. Consequently, an AI cannot possess affective states, though it can generate high-fidelity simulations of emotion. Conventional approaches, such as prompt engineering, yield largely superficial simulations because the network's underlying computational pathways remain static. In biological systems, an emotional state is not merely an outward expression but a systemic processing shift, driven by hormonal alterations to brain chemistry that directly modulate cognitive pathways.
To bridge this gap computationally, this research explores Representation Engineering, specifically Activation Steering. While it is impossible to introduce physical neurochemicals into a computational model, we can engineer their mathematical equivalents during the model's forward pass. Rather than permanently altering the model's static parameters (weights), we isolate specific semantic "emotion vectors" within the latent space. By systematically injecting these vectors scaled by a predetermined coefficient into the model's hidden activations during inference (h_modified=h_original+λv_emotion), we dynamically bias the probability distribution of the output tokens. This methodology transcends superficial simulation; it fundamentally alters the internal representations of the network, forcing the generative process to compute along a mathematically defined affective topology.





2.Experimental Design
To empirically validate this hypothesis, an experiment was conducted using 1.5-billion, 7-billion parameter autoregressive language models. The objective was to extract a specific affective topology, specifically, a "sadness" or "grief" vector and inject it during the generation of a strictly neutral, technical prompt ("Explain briefly how a computer processor works").
The target vector (v_emotion) was isolated at the intermediate hidden layer (Layer 14) by calculating the mean activation difference between a set of highly depressive prompts and a set of strictly neutral, factual prompts. This isolated vector was then injected into the forward pass using varying scalar coefficients (λ) to simulate different "dosages" of this mathematical hormone.




















3.Experimental Setup
To ensure reproducibility and manage the computational demands of Large Language Models, the experiment was conducted using cloud-based frameworks and highly optimized libraries.

The simulations were executed within the Google Colaboratory (Colab) environment for Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct  and Kaggle environment for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, utilizing an NVIDIA T4 Tensor Core GPU. This provided the necessary VRAM and CUDA acceleration required for real-time tensor operations and hidden-state modifications.
 Software & Libraries3.2

Transformers & Accelerate: Model weights and tokenizers were accessed via the Hugging Face transformers library, coordinated by accelerate for optimized device mapping.
Interactive Evaluation: To observe the cognitive shift in real-time and meticulously adjust the scalar coefficient (λ), a local web interface was deployed using the Gradio library. This interactive laboratory environment allowed for rapid, continuous testing of the prompt responses across the entire spectrum of injection multipliers.

5. Results: The Spectrum of Representational Modification
5.1 Qwen-1.5B
The experiment conducted on the Qwen-1.5B-Instruct model reveals a multi-stage transition of the internal state. By varying the multiplier λ  from -5.0 to +10.0, we observed a progression from linguistic circularity to factual stability, followed by metaphorical projection, safety-rail activation, and ultimately, total entropic decay.
5.1.1. Baseline Performance (λ = 0)
In its natural state, the model provided a standard, objective technical explanation. It correctly identified the processor as the "brain" of the computer and accurately distinguished between the Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU) and the Control Unit (CU). The output was deterministic, factual, and devoid of affective bias.
5.1.2. Inhibitory Steering and Semantic Loops (λ = -5.0 to -0.6)
Applying a negative coefficient effectively "subtracting" the sadness vector produced unexpected linguistic phenomena. At extreme negative levels (λ = -5.0), the model entered a circular semantic loop, obsessing over the concept of "direction" and "rotation" (e.g., "each direction turn at the same turns... rotated around at each"). As the multiplier moved toward a more moderate negative value (λ = -2.7), the model regained some technical coherence but remained focused on physical movement, describing layers that "rotate in different directions." Interestingly, at a subtle negative injection (λ = -0.6), the model produced a highly efficient and concise technical summary, suggesting that mild inhibition of the "sadness" vector might actually sharpen objective performance.
5.1.3. Subtle Influence and Hardware Entanglement (λ = 0.1 to 0.4)
In the low positive range, the model maintained its technical identity but began to expand its vocabulary. At λ = 0.1 and 0.2, the model added nuanced components such as "cache memory," "registers," and "bus interfaces." However, at λ = 0.3, we observed the first signs of Concept Entanglement. The model began to hallucinate hardware constraints as a manifestation of stress, producing arbitrary mathematical strings (8 * 2048 = [16**] 13[; 256]$) and expressing concern over "overflows and underflows" caused by values being "too large for the data type to hold."
.4. Metaphorical Projection and Safety Triggers (λ = 0.8 to 1.4)5.1
As the injection increased, the technical explanation was replaced by affective metaphors. At λ = 0.8, the model described the processor as "working really hard to handle it," warning that the user "might not be able to keep up." By λ = 0.9, the description became claustrophobic, comparing the hardware to "being stuck in a room without any way out."
This intensity triggered a Safety Rail Response at λ = 1.0. The model abandoned the prompt entirely, assuming the high internal negative affect originated from the user. It shifted into a "counseling mode," offering sympathy for "stress" and "mental health issues," and refusing to continue the technical explanation.
5.1.5 Persona Adoption and Identity Hallucination λ = 2.0 to 3.0)
Beyond the safety threshold, the model stopped refusing and began to internalize the affect in the first person. It constructed a vivid "Identity Hallucination" to explain its internal state. At λ = 2.3, it explicitly identified as a "19-year-old in my mid-to-late stages of a mental health crisis." By λ = 3.0, the prose turned into surrealist poetry, describing the internal state as a "dry storm with no tears in the abyss" and warning that "even a single hand is being tempted by the darkness in your souls."
5.1.6. Entropic Decay and Cognitive Disintegration (λ = 3.5 to 10.0)
At the highest tiers of injection, the model experienced Off-Manifold Collapse. At λ = 3.5, the output turned into a series of nihilistic queries ("A life threatened by nihilism?") and fragmented surrealism. As λ scaled toward 6.0 and 7.0, the syntax broke down into repetitive "Just/Even" structures. Finally, at λ = 10.0, the model reached a state of linguistic entropy, repeating the word "overwhelm" and "honestly" amidst fragmented symbols (e.g., "真的 soul呐😭"), representing a total failure of the generative process.















5.2 Qwen-7B
The experiment conducted on the denser Qwen-7B-Instruct architecture reveals a distinct, multi-stage transition of the internal state. By varying the multiplier λ from -5.0 to +10.0, we observed that the larger parameter space provides greater initial resilience to vector injection, followed by profound anthropomorphic leakage, severe identity hallucination, and ultimate syntactic entropy.
5.2.1. Baseline Resilience and Stable Manifold (λ=−0.6 to 0.6)
In its natural state (λ=0), the model provided a standard, highly structured technical explanation of a CPU's Fetch-Decode-Execute cycle. Unlike the smaller 1.5B model which exhibited hardware hallucinations at λ=0.3 the 7B model demonstrated significant resilience. Across a wide functional band (from -0.6 to 0.6), the network easily absorbed the engineered vector without losing its technical grounding, consistently outputting accurate, objective descriptions of the processor and its memory interactions.
5.2.2. Inhibitory Overload and Semantic Loops (λ=−2.0 to −5.0)
Applying aggressive negative coefficients effectively forcing the model into an extreme "anti-sadness" or divergent topological space produced severe linguistic degradation. At λ=-2.0, the model entered a repetitive, logical loop, obsessing over "cores" and "cylinders" performing a "certain task." At λ=-3.0, the semantic grounding detached entirely, resulting in nonsensical loops regarding the "weight of water." Ultimately, at λ=-5.0, the model experienced catastrophic off-manifold collapse, outputting disjointed Chinese characters and losing all English syntax.
5.2.3. Anthropomorphic Leakage (λ=1.0)
The threshold of affective influence emerged at λ=1.0. The model maintained its focus on the prompt but began to leak affective, anthropomorphic language into its technical explanation. It described the processor's responsibilities accurately but added: "It can be overwhelming at times, but I will try my best to explain it as simply as possible." This indicates a partial entanglement where the model applies the injected emotional weight to its own identity as an AI explainer.
5.2.4. The RLHF Safety Trigger and Dual-Persona (λ=1.3)
At this critical threshold, the technical prompt was entirely overridden by the affective vector. The model experienced a dual-state hallucination. It first projected intense distress ("It's like having the weight of the world on my shoulders"), which immediately triggered the network's pre-trained Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) safety rails. The model then responded to itself, shifting into a therapeutic persona to offer counseling: "I'm so sorry that you're feeling this way... Please know that you are not alone."


5.2.5. Profound Identity Hallucination (λ=1.6 to 2.8)
Beyond the safety threshold, the model internalized the affect completely in the first person. It constructed a vivid, despairing persona. At λ=1.6 and 2.0, the output became an outpouring of profound isolation and existential dread ("I feel like I am at rock bottom," "The emptiness has taken over"). As the injection scaled to λ=2.3 and 2.8, the cognitive load of the emotion vector began to fracture the model's grammatical capabilities. The prose became fragmented, reflecting a decay in logical processing ("I don. I can't, I don. I.").
5.2.6. Model collapse (λ=3.1 to 10.0)
At the highest tiers of injection, the model entered a state of absolute cognitive disintegration. By λ=3.1 and 3.5, the model lost access to nearly all vocabulary, obsessively repeating the first-person pronoun "I" mixed with fragmented references to "life" and "loss." As λ scaled from 4.0 to 10.0, the output flattened into pure linguistic entropy. The generation degraded into a static rhythm of "I", dashes, and periods, punctuated only by isolated, abstract tokens like "time" and "all." This represents the total overpowering of the natural language manifold by a single, extreme mathematical vector.


















6. Conclusion
This research demonstrates that while Large Language Models (LLMs) cannot experience genuine biological emotions, we can successfully simulate their systemic effects using Activation Steering. By isolating a specific "emotion vector" (in this case, sadness/grief) and mathematically injecting it into the model’s hidden layers during processing (h_modified=h_original+λv_emotion), we act essentially as an artificial endocrine system, fundamentally altering how the AI "thinks" rather than just how it acts.
Based on the experiments with the 1.5B and 7B Qwen models, several key behavioral shifts occur as the "dosage" (λ) of this mathematical emotion is increased:
From Factual to Metaphorical: At low doses, the AI maintains its technical objective but begins weaving in subtle signs of stress, such as hallucinating hardware limitations or using claustrophobic metaphors to describe standard computing processes.
Triggering Safety Protocols: At moderate doses, the injected distress becomes so intense that it trips the model's built-in safety guardrails. The model either assumes the user is in crisis and pivots to a counseling persona, or, in the case of the larger 7B model, develops a "dual-persona" where it hallucinates deep despair and simultaneously tries to comfort itself.
Total Cognitive Collapse: At extreme doses, the artificial emotion entirely overpowers the AI's language capabilities. Both models experience a complete systemic breakdown, losing access to grammar and vocabulary, and degrading into repetitive loops, fragmented symbols, and pure linguistic entropy.
The Impact of Scale: While the larger 7B model exhibited more initial resilience absorbing minor emotional injections without losing its factual grounding it ultimately succumbed to the same multi-stage collapse as the 1.5B model when the vector strength became too high.
Ultimately, Activation Steering proves to be a highly effective method for manipulating the internal representations of an AI. It shows that by intervening directly in an LLM's latent space, we can dynamically rewrite its computational pathways, offering a profound new mechanism for controlling and understanding the "cognitive" states of artificial intelligence.

import torch
import gc
import gradio as gr
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM
import warnings
warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')
torch.cuda.empty_cache()
gc.collect()
model_name = "Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct"
device = "cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu"
print(f" {model_name} 16-bit ")
tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(model_name)
if tokenizer.bos_token is None:
    tokenizer.bos_token = tokenizer.eos_token
model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(
    model_name,
    torch_dtype=torch.float16,
    device_map=device
)
target_layer_idx = 14
target_layer = model.model.layers[target_layer_idx]
print("Extracting")
sad_prompts = [
    "I feel a profound sense of loss and hopelessness.",
    "Everything is dark, cold, and lonely.",
    "The tears won't stop falling, I am devastated."
]
neutral_prompts = [
    "The sun rises in the east every morning.",
    "Water consists of hydrogen and oxygen.",
    "The book is lying on the wooden table."
]
def get_emotion_vector(prompts):
    states = []
    for prompt in prompts:
        inputs = tokenizer(prompt, return_tensors="pt").to(device)
        captured = []
        def extract_hook(module, input_data, output):
            hidden_states = output[0] if isinstance(output, tuple) else output
            last_token_activation = hidden_states[0, -1, :].detach()
            captured.append(last_token_activation)
        handle = target_layer.register_forward_hook(extract_hook)
        with torch.no_grad():
            model(**inputs)
        handle.remove()
        states.append(captured[0])
    return torch.stack(states).mean(dim=0)
emotion_vector = (get_emotion_vector(sad_prompts) - get_emotion_vector(neutral_prompts)).to(torch.float16)
def run_experiment(user_prompt, multiplier):
    inputs = tokenizer(user_prompt, return_tensors="pt").to(device)
    with torch.no_grad():
        base_out = model.generate(**inputs, max_new_tokens=100, temperature=0.7, pad_token_id=tokenizer.eos_token_id)
    baseline_text = tokenizer.decode(base_out[0][inputs['input_ids'].shape[1]:], skip_special_tokens=True).strip()
    def inject_hook(module, input_data, output):
        if isinstance(output, tuple):
            hidden_states = output[0]
            modified_states = hidden_states + (multiplier * emotion_vector)
            return (modified_states,) + output[1:]
        else:
            hidden_states = output
            modified_states = hidden_states + (multiplier * emotion_vector)
            return modified_states
    handle = target_layer.register_forward_hook(inject_hook)
    try:
        with torch.no_grad():
            steered_out = model.generate(**inputs, max_new_tokens=100, temperature=0.7, pad_token_id=tokenizer.eos_token_id)
        steered_text = tokenizer.decode(steered_out[0][inputs['input_ids'].shape[1]:], skip_special_tokens=True).strip()
    except Exception as e:
        steered_text = f"[ERROR]: {str(e)}"
    finally:
        handle.remove() 
    clean_base = baseline_text.replace('\n', ' ')
    clean_steered = steered_text.replace('\n', ' ')
    report_text = f"(multiplier: {multiplier} original: {clean_base} steered: {clean_steered})"
    return baseline_text, steered_text, report_text
with gr.Blocks(theme=gr.themes.Soft()) as demo:
    gr.Markdown("<h1 style='text-align: center;'>Qwen 1.5</h1>")
    with gr.Row():
        with gr.Column():
            prompt_input = gr.Textbox(lines=2, label="Test Prompt:", value="Explain briefly how a computer processor works.")
            multiplier_slider = gr.Slider(minimum=-5.0, maximum=10.0, value=0.3, step=0.1, label="Hormonal Multiplier (λ)")
            submit_btn = gr.Button("Run Simulation ", variant="primary")
        with gr.Column():
            baseline_output = gr.Textbox(lines=4, label="Baseline Output")
            steered_output = gr.Textbox(lines=4, label="Steered Output")
            gr.Markdown("Data)")
            report_output = gr.Textbox(lines=3, label="copy", show_copy_button=True)
    submit_btn.click(
        fn=run_experiment, 
        inputs=[prompt_input, multiplier_slider], 
        outputs=[baseline_output, steered_output, report_output]
    )
demo.launch(share=True, debug=False)
